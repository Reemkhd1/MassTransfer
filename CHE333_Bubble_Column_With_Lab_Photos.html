02 · PROJECT SCOPE

Meet the bubble column

A bubble column is a vertical vessel. Gas enters from the bottom through a sparger, forming bubbles that rise through the liquid.

01Gas inlet
02Sparger
03Gas–liquid contact
04Gas outlet

Why it matters: high contact area, simple construction, and applications in gas absorption, aeration, and bioprocessing.

Trade-off: performance depends on bubble size, flow regime, and mixing.

03 · MECHANISM

What happens at each bubble?

GAS PHASEO₂ inside bubble
INTERFACEO₂ crosses interface
LIQUIDO₂ dissolves in water

Species: oxygen (O₂)

Direction: gas → liquid, while the liquid is undersaturated

Driving force: the difference between saturation and bulk dissolved oxygen, C* − C.

Initial assumptions: well-mixed bulk liquid; constant temperature and pressure for a given run; negligible oxygen consumption for the basic nonreactive model.

04 · PRELIMINARY EQUATIONS

Only two equations to start

EQUILIBRIUM · THERMODYNAMICS
C* = Hcp pO₂

Henry’s law gives the equilibrium dissolved oxygen concentration at the gas partial pressure.

Hcp: solubility-form Henry coefficient · pO₂: oxygen partial pressure.

TRANSFER RATE · MASS TRANSFER
dC/dt = kLa(C* − C)

The model tracks how dissolved oxygen approaches equilibrium over time.

kLa: volumetric mass-transfer coefficient · C: bulk DO · C*: equilibrium DO.

We will use these as a preliminary nonreactive oxygen–water model.
